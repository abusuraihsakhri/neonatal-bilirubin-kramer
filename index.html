Kramer zones

Record the extent of visible jaundice, using the cephalocaudal Kramer description. This tool documents an observation; it does not estimate bilirubin concentration or determine treatment.

Visual assessment is insufficient. Jaundice may be difficult to detect in darker skin tones. Suspected jaundice requires objective bilirubin measurement per local guidelines. Visible jaundice within the first 24 hours warrants urgent clinical assessment and serum bilirubin measurement.

Kramer distribution reference

  1. 1 Head and neck
  2. 2 Upper trunk — chest and upper abdomen
  3. 3 Lower abdomen and thighs
  4. 4 Arms and lower legs
  5. 5 Palms and soles

Skin distribution has substantial interobserver variability and is not a substitute for transcutaneous bilirubin (TcB) or total serum bilirubin (TSB). Treatment decisions require hour-specific, gestation-specific clinical guidance and measured bilirubin.

Privacy: Assessment runs entirely in this browser. No data is sent to a server, stored in browser storage, or transmitted for analysis. JSON export is generated locally.

Clinical references: NICE CG98 and AAP 2022 hyperbilirubinemia guideline. For education and documentation, not autonomous medical decision-making.
